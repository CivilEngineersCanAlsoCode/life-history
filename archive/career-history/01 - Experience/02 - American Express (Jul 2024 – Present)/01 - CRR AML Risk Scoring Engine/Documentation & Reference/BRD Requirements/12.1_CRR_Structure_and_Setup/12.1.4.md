# 12.1.4 — Risk Rating Calculation

**Parent:** 12.1 CRR Structure and Setup
**Status:** In Development

## Requirement
- The overall risk is determined by summing all Risk Points
- A threshold will be used to assign the corresponding risk rating

## Key Concept
Total CRR Score = Sum of all (Weighting × Multiplier) across all risk elements. The score is then mapped to a 1-10 scale, which maps to Risk Classes (Low/Medium/High/Prohibited).

## Complete Scoring Flow (Confirmed from Coaching)

### Step 1: Rule-Level — Multiplier Resolution
```
Multiple rules can trigger under a single Risk Element
├── MAXIMUM multiplier taken (for most elements)
└── MINIMUM multiplier taken (for Length of Relationship, Acquisition Channel)
    └── Reason: Inverse relationship with risk
        - Longer relationship → more trust → less risky
        - Older acquisition channel → more data → less risky
```

### Step 2: Element-Level — Risk Points
```
Risk Points = Weighting × Resolved Multiplier
```
- Weighting = static importance factor (0-900 standard; 4000 for HRO override)
- Multiplier = resolved from Step 1 (value-based: 0-99 or FA-based: 1-10)

### Step 3: Category-Level — Category Score
```
Category Score = Sum of all Risk Points within that category
```

### Step 4: 3-Tier Roll-Up (Max Contagion Principle)
```
Step 4a: PURE CUSTOMER SCORE
  - Customer has multiple accounts
  - Select MOST RISKY risk elements across ALL accounts
  - Calculate Pure Customer Score from these elements

Step 4b: HIERARCHY SCORE
  - Calculate individual account scores
  - Maximum account score → Hierarchy Score
  - If multiple hierarchies → Maximum hierarchy score

Step 4c: FINAL CUSTOMER SCORE
  - Compare: Pure Customer Score vs Hierarchy Customer Score
  - HIGHER of the two = Final Customer Score
```

### Step 5: Risk Class Assignment
```
Final Customer Score → mapped via Enterprise Thresholds → Risk Rating (1-10)

| Rating | Risk Class  |
|--------|------------|
| 1-3    | Low        |
| 4-6    | Medium     |
| 7-9    | High       |
| 10     | Prohibited |
```

### Special Overrides
- **HRO (Weight 4000):** Bypasses entire calculation → auto-sets score to 9 (High)
- **ARF Levels:** Cumulative impact (Low ~300, Medium ~600, High ~900 weight)
- **Default Multiplier:** Applied when data is unavailable (fallback scoring)

## Why Max Contagion Design
- **Conservative approach:** In AML, false negatives (missing a risky customer) are far more dangerous than false positives
- If even ONE account is high-risk, the entire customer is treated as high-risk
- Riskiest signal is NEVER diluted by averaging across low-risk accounts
- Regulatory expectation: suspicious activity in ANY account should flag the entire customer relationship

## Discovery Questions (Answered)
- **Linear addition or weighted averages?** → Linear addition (sum of risk points). No averaging — this ensures high-risk elements aren't diluted.
- **Dynamic threshold by customer segment?** → Enterprise thresholds are standard; Markets can override locally. 80/10/10 rule used for threshold determination.
