## 05. Metrics & North Star

- What was your North Star metric and how did you define it?
- What leading and lagging indicators did you track?
- How did you measure success?
- What was your framework for prioritization?

**What was the North Star and how did you define it?**
The North Star was **"Regulatory Compliance Velocity"** — time-to-deploy rule changes from request to production in a secure, auditable manner with zero compliance violations. We decomposed this into: (1) Days-to-configure a rule change (target: 1 day from analyst request to approval); (2) Sandbox test coverage (target: 100% of production changes tested in sandbox before production); (3) Audit trail completeness (target: 100% of rule changes logged with maker, checker, timestamp, and rationale); (4) Sub-2-second onboarding SLA compliance (target: P99 latency ≤ 2 seconds). This North Star aligned with both user needs (faster compliance response) and regulatory requirements (explainability, auditability, zero-error tolerance).

**What leading and lagging indicators did you track?**
Leading indicators: (1) Sandbox adoption rate (% of analysts using sandbox before production deployment — target: 80%+); (2) Configuration UI usability score (SUS score from compliance practitioners — target: 70+); (3) Maker-checker approval time (hours from submission to approval — target: <4 hours); (4) Rule change throughput (rules deployed per PI cycle); (5) Test coverage (% of rule configurations validated in sandbox). Lagging indicators: (1) Production incidents caused by rule configuration (target: zero); (2) Regulatory audit findings related to rule documentation or traceability (target: zero); (3) Time-to-respond to regulatory guidance changes (measured in days); (4) User satisfaction (NPS from compliance practitioners); (5) Operational toil (hours/week spent on manual overrides or IT requests).

**How did you measure success?**
Success came in three forms: User success — [VERIFY: 50%] improvement in usability (measured via SUS scores and task completion times in UX research); Operational success — delivered Phase 1 (7 of 10 Rally capabilities) through modular capability delivery within the 3-year regulatory timeline, with Phase 2 in progress; Regulatory success — achieved zero non-conformances in pilot audit of GCIP configuration and scoring audit trails. We also tracked qualitative success: stakeholder confidence. After initial skepticism, adoption of sandbox testing and self-service configuration became the norm among compliance analysts, indicating confidence in the new platform.

**What was your prioritization framework?**
I used a weighted-scoring model: [VERIFY: were these the actual weights?] **(Regulatory Risk Impact × 3) + (Operational Efficiency × 2) + (User Pain Reduction × 1) + (Future AI/ML Enablement × 1) = Priority Score**. Regulatory requirements (maker-checker, audit trails, explainability) were weighted 3× because AML is zero-mistake-tolerance. Operational efficiency (sandbox, versioning) was weighted 2× because it directly unblocked compliance teams' ability to self-serve. User pain reduction (UI/UX) was weighted 1× because it amplified adoption. AI/ML enablement was weighted 1× because the BRD called for future directional guidance toward ML-based models. This framework prevented shiny-feature bias and kept the team focused on high-impact, high-urgency work.
