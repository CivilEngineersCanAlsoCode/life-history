## 03. Discovery & Validation

- How did you discover the most impactful opportunities?
- What did your discovery phase reveal about the business?
- How did you validate assumptions?
- What changed based on what you learned?

**How did you discover high-impact opportunities?**
I started by mapping the end-to-end AML risk scoring workflow: rule ingestion → configuration → sandbox testing → approval → production deployment → monitoring. At each stage, I identified "time drains" and "error vectors." Using the Kano model, I categorized features: (1) Basic hygiene (maker-checker controls, audit trails — regulatory table stakes); (2) Performance (reducing configuration time, sandbox validation — operational efficiency); (3) Delighters (advanced rule composition, real-time monitoring dashboard, sandbox versioning). I then scored opportunities on: regulatory risk mitigation, operational efficiency gains, and user pain reduction. The highest-impact items were sandbox + version control (addressed production risk), self-service UI (reduced IT dependency), and centralized list management (eliminated manual overrides).

**What did discovery reveal about the business?**
A critical insight: Cadence wasn't just a risk scoring engine—it was an unintended data warehouse. [VERIFY: approximate number] downstream consumers (KYC, Transaction Monitoring, EDD, Anomaly Index) depended on its data schemas and APIs. This meant the modernization couldn't be a simple migration; it required building backward-compatible APIs and a phased cutover strategy. Additionally, AML compliance is zero-mistake-tolerance: every deployment risk had regulatory implications. This mandated obsessive attention to deployment safety, auditability, and rollback mechanisms—not just velocity.

**How did you validate assumptions?**
I ran three validation streams: (1) **User testing** — paper prototypes and Figma wireframes with 8+ compliance practitioners to validate sandbox UX and configuration workflows; (2) **Regulatory validation** — reviewed audit reports, regulatory guidance, and industry best practices (FinCEN, FATF) to ensure GCIP architecture met explainability and auditability requirements; (3) **Technical validation** — worked with the engineering team to stress-test sub-2-second SLA assumptions at 30M TPS scale and validate GCP deployment architecture (Lumi Projects, BigQuery, Cloud SQL, PubSub). User feedback directly shaped iterations on the Rule Configuration UI.

**What changed based on discovery?**
Initial delivery plan was phased across the 3-year regulatory timeline. Through discovery, I identified opportunities for aggressive Phase 1 scoping — delivering 7 of 10 Rally capabilities early through modular capability delivery rather than waiting for a complete system cutover. Additionally, user research revealed that compliance teams had deep domain expertise but lacked technical vocabulary to describe their needs. This drove the decision to co-design the UI with compliance practitioners rather than engineering-first design. The roadmap also shifted to prioritize explainability features (rule impact preview, scoring rationale) earlier than originally planned, based on regulatory feedback.
