## 06. AI/ML Depth (When Relevant)

- Is AI/ML a core part of your product strategy? In what form?
- What are the key challenges (data quality, explainability, regulatory compliance)?
- How are you thinking about the future of AI/ML in your domain?
- What are you doing NOW to lay the foundation?

**Is AI/ML a core part of the future roadmap?**
Yes. The BRD explicitly directs future architecture toward AI/ML-based AML risk scoring (BRD Section 12.19). Currently, CRR uses rule-based logic: weighted risk categories, multipliers, and thresholds refined by compliance expertise. The modernization to GCIP creates the foundational platform to support eventual ML-driven scoring (e.g., anomaly detection, pattern recognition, propensity models for customer risk classification). The current rule-based engine isn't being replaced—it's being preserved while we build the data quality, monitoring, and explainability infrastructure that ML models will require.

**What are the key challenges in transitioning to AI/ML?**
Four critical challenges: (1) **Explainability and regulatory compliance** — AML regulators (FinCEN, FATF) require that every scoring decision be auditable and explainable to customers and regulators. ML models (especially deep learning) are "black boxes." We need interpretable ML techniques (SHAP values, feature importance decomposition, decision trees as surrogates) that provide regulatory-grade explainability; (2) **Data quality** — AML ML models require clean, labeled training data. Cadence's fragmented data from 30+ sources creates quality challenges: inconsistent data schemas, missing values, label bias (most customers are legitimate, making it hard to train fraud detectors); (3) **Drift and monitoring** — ML models degrade when real-world distributions shift (e.g., new money laundering techniques emerge). We need continuous monitoring and retraining pipelines to maintain model performance; (4) **Regulatory approval and validation** — deploying an ML model for AML requires pre-approval from regulators. We'd need rigorous backtesting, stress-testing, and documentation to demonstrate the model's fairness, stability, and performance across demographics and geographies.

**How are you thinking about the future of AI/ML?**
The vision is a **hybrid human-in-the-loop system**: ML models identify high-risk patterns and anomalies; human compliance experts validate and override; feedback loop retrains models. Benefits: ML catches novel patterns humans miss; humans catch edge cases and regulatory nuances ML models miss. Near-term (12–18 months): build explainability and monitoring infrastructure, experiment with interpretable ML models (logistic regression, decision trees, XGBoost) on low-risk use cases, and gather regulatory feedback on compliance expectations. Medium-term (18–36 months): deploy production ML models for specific risk categories (e.g., transaction anomaly detection), measure performance against rule-based baselines, and gather MLRO feedback. Long-term (3+ years): evolve toward automated risk scoring with human oversight, continuous retraining, and regulatory partnerships on ML governance.

**What are you doing NOW to lay the foundation?**
Three concrete initiatives: (1) **Data foundation** — building GCIP's data layer with standardized schemas across 30+ sources, improving data quality monitoring, and creating labeled datasets for future ML experiments; (2) **Explainability architecture** — every rule execution logs decision rationale (which rules fired, weights, multipliers, final score). This audit trail enables us to calculate feature importance and explain any scoring decision; (3) **Monitoring and governance** — building dashboards to track model performance, data drift, and regulatory compliance. We're also documenting GCIP's explainability capabilities for upcoming regulatory discussions, positioning Amex as a leader in responsible, compliant AI for AML.
