## 10. Execution & Delivery

- How did you manage the delivery timeline and team?
- What was your prioritization and trade-off process?
- How did you handle scope creep and complexity?
- What was your key execution insight or breakthrough?

**How did you manage the delivery timeline and team?**
The organization operates under SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework) with two-week sprints and bi-weekly PI (Program Increment) planning. I managed two scrum teams: **Rule Configuration team** (~12 members: frontend engineers, backend engineers, QA) building the self-service UI and configuration APIs, and **Rule Execution team** (~6 members: backend engineers, data engineers) building the scoring engine and data ingestion layer. My role spanned both teams as the product manager, owning the roadmap, prioritization, and stakeholder alignment. **Key execution practices**: (1) **Daily standups** with both teams to identify blockers early; (2) **Weekly prioritization meetings** with the engineering lead to align engineering capacity with business priorities; (3) **Bi-weekly stakeholder reviews** showing incremental progress to the Director of Compliance and compliance practitioners; (4) **Monthly retrospectives** to identify process improvements. This tight feedback loop prevented misalignment and caught issues early.

**What was your prioritization and trade-off process?**
I used the weighted-scoring framework described in Metrics & North Star: Regulatory impact weighted 3× (because AML is zero-mistake-tolerance), operational efficiency 2×, user satisfaction 1×, and future AI/ML enablement 1×. This framework prevented emotional decisions and kept teams focused on high-impact work. When features competed for engineering capacity, we scored them and explicitly communicated why Feature X ranked above Feature Y. Example trade-off: **Advanced rule composition vs. monitoring dashboard**. Both were requested. Scoring: advanced composition (regulatory impact 3 + efficiency 2 = 5 points) vs. dashboard (efficiency 2 + future vision 1 = 3 points). We built advanced composition first, explaining to stakeholders why it unblocked more value. The dashboard was backlogged but not deprioritized—just deferred.

**How did you handle scope creep and complexity?**
Scope creep is fatal in compliance projects because changes can introduce regulatory risk. I implemented **strict change control**: (1) **MVP definition** — before each PI, we defined a minimal viable product (e.g., "sandbox supports single-rule testing"). Anything beyond MVP was a "future enhancement"; (2) **Regulatory compliance gate** — any change request was evaluated for compliance impact. If it touched rule logic or audit trails, it required the Director of Compliance's approval; (3) **Capacity planning** — I allocated 80% of sprint capacity to committed roadmap items and reserved 20% for bugs/technical debt. This prevented unexpected delays and gave flexibility for urgent items; (4) **Transparent backlog** — the full backlog was visible to all stakeholders with clear prioritization. When someone requested a feature, they could see where it ranked and why. This transparency reduced complaints because stakeholders understood the trade-offs. Example: A compliance manager requested "real-time rule impact simulation." We scored it high (efficiency 3), but it required 3 sprints. We committed to PI 3 (6 weeks out), managing expectations upfront.

**What was your key execution insight or breakthrough?**
The breakthrough was reframing "modernization" as **"modular capability delivery."** Instead of treating GCIP as one monolithic system to be delivered at the end of the 3-year regulatory timeline, we broke it into independent capabilities delivered incrementally within PI cycles: (1) **PI 1**: Sandbox testing (let analysts test in isolation) + self-service rule configuration UI; (2) **PI 2**: Advanced rule composition (complex boolean logic) + centralized list management + maker-checker controls + audit trail hardening. Each capability delivered immediate user value and regulatory improvement. We didn't wait for a "complete system" — we shipped working capabilities every 2 weeks. Phase 1 delivered 7 of 10 Rally capabilities; Phase 2 is in progress. This prevented analysis paralysis, kept teams focused, and gave compliance teams confidence through incremental wins. The key insight: **in regulated environments, shipping incrementally with regulatory gates at each stage is safer and faster than big-bang migrations**.
