## Q4. Solution Architecture & Trade-offs

**How does the Unified Sandbox Journey work end-to-end?**

User creates a sandbox with Enterprise or Market scope (mutual exclusion enforced—no overlapping sandboxes). Sandbox enters Draft state. User navigates to Rules, Assets, or Fundamental Assessment sub-tabs within the sandbox context, makes edits (all accumulate in Draft state, uncommitted). Submit for Simulation: triggers a modal requiring change summary and business justification. Backend creates an immutable version snapshot. Simulation job executes on GCP using a production data copy, applying sandbox config changes, and rescoring a representative customer population. Real-time progress tracking allows cancellation mid-simulation. Results display in tabular format: customer cohorts, risk score deltas, distribution changes. User reviews results and submits for approval. Two-step approval: first approver (user A) reviews and approves; second approver (different user) from MLRO/MCO reviews and approves (BRD 12.10 maker-checker). Upon final approval, atomic promotion merges all changes (Rules + Assets + FA) in single database transaction. Enterprise asset updates propagate to all markets. Approved version transitions to Promoted state. Full rollback capability: select any previous version, create new version from that baseline (non-editable states) or overwrite Draft state uncommitted changes.

**Why is mutual exclusion a necessary constraint?**

Enterprise and Market sandboxes cannot coexist simultaneously. Rationale: prevent conflicting changes. Example—Enterprise analyst updates a global asset; Market analyst simultaneously updates the same asset at market scope. If both promote, atomicity breaks. Mutual exclusion eliminates this race condition. Trade-off: analysts must serialize their work (coordinate across teams). Benefit: zero merge conflicts, zero rollback complexity.

**Why is atomic promotion critical (regulatory and technical)?**

Rules, Assets, and Fundamental Assessment are interdependent. If Rules promote but Assets fail, customer scoring is broken (inconsistent state). Atomic promotion ensures: all-or-nothing semantics. If any component fails during promotion, entire transaction rolls back. Technical benefit: no partial state corruption. Regulatory benefit: audit trail shows clean before/after states; no ambiguity about what changed when.

**What are the key trade-offs?**

Trade-off 1: Mutual exclusion vs. parallel work. Benefit: conflict prevention. Cost: serialized workflows across teams. Mitigation: clear sandbox scheduling (sprints, change calendar). Trade-off 3: Full rollback capability vs. audit immutability. Benefit: recovery from mistakes. Cost: must maintain version history indefinitely. Mitigation: archive old versions after [VERIFY: add retention policy]. Trade-off 4: Two-step approval vs. single-step. Benefit: BRD 12.10 compliance, reduced errors. Cost: approval cycle time. Mitigation: optimized UI to reduce approval turnaround.
