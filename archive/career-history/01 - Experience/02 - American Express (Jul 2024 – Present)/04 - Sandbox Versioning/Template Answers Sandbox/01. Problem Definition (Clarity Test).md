## Q1. Problem Definition (Clarity Test)

**What specific pain point or constraint triggered this initiative, and how do you frame it?**

The CRR (Customer Risk Rating) platform powered risk-decisioning across 40+ markets and 30M+ daily transactions, yet operated without version control, simulation capabilities, or atomic changes. Compliance analysts had no sandbox environment to test configuration changes before promotion. Changes to Rules, Assets, or Fundamental Assessment flowed directly to production—irreversible without manual IT intervention. A single misconfigured rule could shift risk classifications for millions of customers, triggering regulatory exposure: false positives in AML monitoring, missed SAR filings, or undetected bad actors. BRD 12.10 mandated maker-checker governance (two-step approval from different users), BRD 12.8 required sandbox/simulation functionality, and BRD 12.12 demanded real-time rescoring at onboarding. The platform's migration from Cadence to GCIP on GCP cloud created a modernization window—an opportunity to embed these controls natively rather than bolt them on.

**How does this problem manifest for end users?**

Compliance analysts feared configuration changes. Without simulation, they couldn't preview impact. Without version history, they couldn't trace why a rule behaved differently. Without approval workflows, single actors could inadvertently misconfigure risk thresholds, causing harm to customer populations. MLRO teams (viewer role) couldn't validate that changes aligned with risk appetite. Rollback scenarios required escalations to IT, introducing delays when rapid remediation was needed.

**Why now? What changed in the competitive or regulatory landscape?**

GCIP on GCP cloud enabled stateless sandbox architecture. Regulatory momentum around real-time transaction monitoring (BRD 12.8, 12.9.4, 12.10) demanded proof of controlled, auditable change management. Cadence's limitations made this infeasible; GCP's design enabled it.
