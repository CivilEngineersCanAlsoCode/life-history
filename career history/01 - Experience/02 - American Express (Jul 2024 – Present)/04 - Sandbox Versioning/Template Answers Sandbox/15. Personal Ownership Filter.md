## Q15. Personal Ownership Filter

**How did you personally contribute to defining and delivering this feature?**

I owned the full lifecycle: problem discovery through launch readiness. Discovery: conducted 6+ interviews with the compliance team across 3 markets to understand current pain points and regulatory constraints. Shadowed a real rule change incident where a misconfigured rule went to production and required emergency IT rollback. Reviewed regulatory requirements (BRD 12.8, 12.10, 12.12) and mapped them to technical solutions. User story writing: I authored all 11 stories with detailed AC covering state transitions, error scenarios, edge cases, and regulatory compliance proof points. Design collaboration: I led design reviews with backend/frontend engineers, challenged assumptions, and ensured acceptance criteria were testable. Prototype testing: I facilitated low-fidelity prototype walkthroughs with analysts and MLRO teams; their feedback directly informed AC refinement. Scope management: I pushed back on scope creep (e.g., "Can we add conditional approval logic in Phase 1?") and justified Phase 2 roadmap. Regulatory alignment: I personally reviewed each story's AC against BRD requirements to ensure compliance artifacts (audit trail, maker-checker) were non-negotiable.

**What was your biggest personal challenge, and how did you overcome it?**

Challenge: Balancing regulatory rigor (BRD 12.10 maker-checker is non-negotiable) with user experience (approvals can slow down rule updates). Engineers initially resisted two-step approval, fearing approval bottlenecks would arise. My response: (a) user research showing typical approval cycle time is <2 hours (acceptable), (b) proposed escalation path for urgent changes (fast-track approval if MCO directs it), (c) prototype showing approval UI is dead simple (two checkboxes, add comment, click Approve). This shifted the narrative from "approval is a burden" to "approval is a control we can optimize." Engineers bought in.

**What decision are you most proud of in this feature design?**

The mutual exclusion constraint. This is not intuitive—it seems like a limitation. But it's brilliant: by preventing concurrent Enterprise and Market sandboxes, we eliminate merge conflicts entirely. This means atomic promotion is feasible. This means zero rollback complexity. Engineers initially disliked it (wanting to maximize parallelization), but once I explained the conflict scenarios and showed that serialization is acceptable (analysts can schedule work), they agreed it's the right call. I'm proud because it's a subtle design choice that dramatically simplifies the system and eliminates whole classes of bugs.

**What would you do differently in hindsight?**

Two things: (a) I would have started simulation SLA negotiation with GCP platform team earlier (Month 2 instead of Month 4). This would have surfaced simulation latency concerns earlier and allowed more time for optimization. (b) I would have run a longer pilot with the compliance team (4 weeks instead of 1 week) before hard launch. More pilot time would have caught nuances in approval workflows and reduced post-launch friction. Both are learnings for next feature.

**How does this feature reflect your product philosophy?**

This feature embodies my core belief: constraints are features. Mutual exclusion, atomic promotion, two-step approval—these are constraints. They're also the mechanisms that prevent harm. I design for "safe complexity"—systems that are complex enough to be powerful, but constrained enough to be safe. Sandbox Versioning achieves this: analysts have flexibility (edit all config types), but within safety rails (approval, simulation, rollback). No single actor can break production. This is product design in regulated domains: maximize capability within compliance constraints.

**Who else deserves credit for this feature's success?**

The Director of Compliance and his team for their patience in user research and prototype feedback. The GCP platform team for committing to simulation SLA and building robust APIs. Backend engineers for thoughtful atomic promotion design. Frontend engineers for intuitive state management and approval UX. The compliance/risk leadership team for clarifying regulatory requirements (BRDs) and making sandbox non-optional. And the Quality team for comprehensive UAT planning. This is a team effort; my role was orchestration and advocacy.
