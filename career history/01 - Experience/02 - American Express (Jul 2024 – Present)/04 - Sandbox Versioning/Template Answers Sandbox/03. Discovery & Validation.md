## Q3. Discovery & Validation

**How did you validate that this was the right problem to solve?**

Interviewed the compliance team across three markets, observing end-to-end rule change workflows. Observed: (a) analysts emailing rule changes to IT for manual configuration, (b) IT applying changes without simulation—pure black-box promotion, (c) regulatory audits flagging missing change justifications and approval evidence, (d) post-incident root cause analysis revealing single-actor configuration errors. Reviewed audit logs and found [VERIFY: add specific incident or audit gap]. Mapped BRD 12.8, 12.9.4, 12.10, 12.12 to current state and identified compliance gaps. Conducted a regulatory impact assessment: concluded that the sandbox is not a "nice-to-have nice" feature—it's a mandatory control to avoid regulatory fines and customer harm.

**What did you learn from users about their actual vs. stated needs?**

Stated need: "We need a sandbox." Actual need: "We need to feel confident that rule changes won't break production, and we need proof of approval for audits." Users didn't ask for atomic promotion or rollback—they asked for "undo." Atomic promotion and rollback emerged as the engineering solution to that need. MLRO teams stated they wanted "view" access; discovery revealed they actually needed "approve/reject" access to risk-critical changes. BRD 12.9.4 reflected this deeper need.

**How did you test product-market fit before full build?**

Designed a low-fidelity prototype (Figma wireframes) showing sandbox creation, draft editing, submit workflow, simulation modal, and approval chain. Walked through with the compliance team and MLRO representatives. Observed confusion around "what gets simulated" and "when is rollback possible"—refined acceptance criteria accordingly. Tested the mutual exclusion constraint (Enterprise and Market sandboxes cannot coexist): users found this restrictive but understood the reasoning (conflict prevention). No user rejected the overall design; all validated the need.
