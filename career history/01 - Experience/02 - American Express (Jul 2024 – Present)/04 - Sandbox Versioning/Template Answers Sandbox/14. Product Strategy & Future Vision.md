## Q14. Product Strategy & Future Vision

**What does Year 2 roadmap look like for Sandbox Versioning?**

Year 2 Phase 1 (Quarters 1-2, post-launch): Advanced approval workflows. Current: two-step approval (Approver A, Approver B). Future: conditional approval based on change risk (low-risk changes = single approver, high-risk changes = three approvers), risk-based escalation (changes impacting >100k customers auto-escalate to MCO), approval SLA enforcement (notify manager if pending >X hours). Year 2 Phase 2 (Quarters 3-4): Simulation analytics. Current: basic summary (X% of customers moved to High risk). Future: machine learning models predicting approval cycle time, identifying high-risk rule changes early, recommending approval alternatives. Year 2 Phase 3 (Year 2+): Integration with fraud/AML orchestration. Current: sandbox is isolated to CRR. Future: when analyst updates CRR rules that impact fraud detection, simulation includes fraud impact (e.g., "This rule change will reduce fraud detection accuracy by 2%"). This requires cross-platform simulation.

**How does Sandbox Versioning relate to broader CRR modernization?**

Sandbox is Feature 1 in CRR 2.0. Downstream features depend on it: Feature 2 (ML-powered Fundamental Assessment) requires sandbox versioning for model updates. Feature 3 (Real-time rescoring at onboarding, BRD 12.12) requires sandbox-tested rules. Feature 4 (Audit reporting) consumes the audit trail from sandbox. Sandbox is the foundation for all downstream CRR features.

**What is the vision for version 2.0 of the sandbox?**

Vision: From "sandbox for configuration changes" to "sandbox for risk experimentation platform." Imagine: analyst creates a sandbox not just to fix a bug, but to A/B test two different rule sets against production customer population, comparing outcomes. Sandbox becomes a platform for risk simulations, not just configuration promotion. This requires: (a) multi-version comparison (show results from Version A vs. Version B side-by-side), (b) statistical significance testing (is observed risk score delta statistically significant?), (c) impact estimation (what will this change cost us in fraud losses vs. prevent in bad actors?). This is Year 3+ vision, dependent on Fundamental Assessment maturity and analytics infrastructure.

**How do you measure product-market fit evolving over time?**

Month 1: adoption rate (% of analysts using sandbox). Month 3: approval cycle time stability (approaching target SLA). Month 6: advanced features adoption (% using rollback, % using audit trail export). Month 12: user satisfaction (NPS score from analysts [VERIFY: add target NPS, likely 50+]). Year 2: expansion to adjacent use cases (e.g., sandbox for Asset management, not just Rules). These metrics track not just adoption, but deepening engagement.

**What are the strategic risks to sandbox success long-term?**

Risk 1: Approval bottleneck. If approval cycle time exceeds [VERIFY: add tolerance, likely 4 hours], analysts circumvent the sandbox (go directly to IT for changes). Mitigation: monitor SLA; if breached, add parallel approval or escalation paths. Risk 2: Simulation irrelevance. If simulation results are consistently ignored by approvers (rubber-stamped), sandbox loses credibility. Mitigation: training, culture shift, highlight cases where simulation prevented incidents. Risk 3: Technical debt. If sandbox infrastructure is not maintained, simulation latency increases, reliability decreases. Mitigation: allocate 20% of team capacity to technical debt; monitor SLA trends; refresh GCP queries annually.

**How do you position sandbox for future regulatory changes?**

Current regulatory drivers: BRD 12.8, 12.10, 12.12, audit trails. Likely future drivers: real-time impact reporting (regulators want live dashboards showing risk profile changes), federated approval (approvers across multiple jurisdictions), explainability (why did risk score change—attribution required). Sandbox design should be extensible: audit trail format allows future fields (e.g., regulatory jurisdiction, impact attribution). Approval workflow allows future steps. Simulation results allow future enrichment (explainability scores). This forward-thinking design prevents future rewrites.
