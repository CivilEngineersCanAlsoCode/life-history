## 02. Customer & Persona Depth

- Who are the key user personas?
- What are their success metrics and daily workflows?
- How did you validate their needs?
- What did you learn that surprised you?

**Who are the key personas and what are their jobs-to-be-done?**
Four primary personas emerged: (1) **Compliance Analyst** — responsible for monitoring customer risk profiles and recommending rule adjustments based on regulatory guidance; (2) **Compliance Manager** — oversees analysts, approves rule changes, and ensures maker-checker controls are enforced; (3) **MLRO (Money Laundering Reporting Officer)** — accountable for AML regulatory compliance and needs full auditability of risk scoring decisions; (4) **Operational Risk Team** — manages rule versioning, sandbox testing, and production deployments. Each persona had distinct needs: analysts needed intuitive configuration UI, managers needed approval workflows, MLROs needed comprehensive audit trails, and operators needed safe deployment mechanisms.

**What were their workflows and success metrics?**
Compliance analysts spent significant time manually documenting rule changes, waiting for IT to code updates, and then discovering in production that scoring behavior was unexpected. Success for them meant reducing time-to-configure from [VERIFY: baseline cycle time] to self-service minutes. Managers success metric was visibility into pending approvals and full traceability of who changed what and when. MLROs measured success by "100% auditability"—every rule change, test, approval, and deployment logged immutably. Operators needed to deploy rule changes within a PI cycle (2 weeks) without incident.

**How did you validate these needs?**
I conducted 20+ UX research sessions with compliance practitioners, shadowing analysts in their daily work, observing configuration workflows in Cadence, and facilitating working sessions with the compliance leadership team. I used task analysis to map current-state friction points: where users got stuck, where they resorted to workarounds, and where regulatory controls were manually enforced outside the system. I also reviewed regulatory audit reports to understand compliance gaps.

**What surprised you?**
The biggest insight: compliance practitioners weren't asking for a "new platform"—they were asking for trust. Users had workarounds and manual processes they'd developed over years. Resistance to change came not from feature requests being ignored, but from fear that a new system wouldn't maintain the regulatory rigor they'd built through workarounds. This reframed my stakeholder engagement strategy toward demonstrating that GCIP + new features enhanced, not diminished, regulatory control. The secondary surprise: analysts had never tested rule changes before production. Introducing a sandbox was genuinely novel to their workflow.
