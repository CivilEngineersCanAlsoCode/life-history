## 01. Problem Definition (Clarity Test)

- What was the core problem you were solving?
- Who had the problem and what was the quantified impact?
- Why hadn't this been solved before? What were the constraints?
- How did you frame the opportunity differently than the status quo?

**What was the core problem?**
American Express needed to modernize its 12–14 year old AML risk scoring platform (Cadence) that was originally architected as a data store, not a purpose-built compliance engine. The system had been repurposed for unrelated workloads (reporting, account review, regulatory submissions), creating technical debt, slow deployment cycles, and fragile rule configuration processes. The key user pain point: compliance teams could not self-serve rule configuration changes. Every rule update required hard-coding through technical development cycles with no sandbox to validate changes before production, creating operational bottlenecks and regulatory risk.

**Who had the problem and what was the impact?**
The business problem spanned two audiences: (1) Compliance practitioners (analysts, managers, MLRO teams) who experienced tedious, time-consuming configuration workflows and (2) Enterprise Amex leadership managing 30M+ daily transactions across 40+ markets facing regulatory pressure to demonstrate explainable, auditable AML controls. The legacy system lacked version control, centralized list management, and sandbox capabilities—all regulatory requirements under AML frameworks. No quantified metrics were available, but the configuration delays directly impacted the organization's ability to respond to new regulatory guidance or customer risk profile changes in real-time, with sub-2-second onboarding SLA pressures.

**Why hadn't this been solved before? What were the constraints?**
Cadence's foundational architecture—built as a data warehouse, not a compliance engine—made it architecturally misaligned with the product's true purpose. The system had accumulated [VERIFY: approximate number] unrelated workloads, creating stakeholder complexity. Additionally, the organization lacked bandwidth to undertake a greenfield modernization while operating a live production system serving enterprise-wide compliance functions. Technical debt and regulatory requirements had to be managed concurrently.

**How did you frame the opportunity?**
Rather than a pure technical migration, I positioned this as a compliance capability transformation. The narrative shifted from "legacy system update" to "enabling compliance teams as first-class product users through self-service, explainable rule configuration." This framing aligned engineering investment with business stakeholder goals (regulatory compliance, operational efficiency, explainability) and elevated the initiative's strategic importance.
