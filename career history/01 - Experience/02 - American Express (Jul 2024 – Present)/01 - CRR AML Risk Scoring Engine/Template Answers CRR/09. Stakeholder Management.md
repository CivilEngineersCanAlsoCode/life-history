## 09. Stakeholder Management

- Who were your key stakeholders and what did each need?
- How did you align competing interests?
- What was your communication strategy?
- How did you build trust?

**Who were the key stakeholders and what did each need?**
**Primary stakeholders**: (1) **Director of Compliance** — needed assurance that GCIP was more compliant and auditable than Cadence, and that rule deployment would accelerate without regulatory risk; (2) **Engineering Lead** — needed a clear technical roadmap, resource allocation, and confidence that the modernization was architecturally sound; (3) **Compliance analysts and managers** — needed a usable, trustworthy UI that actually saved them time; (4) **MLRO teams** (Money Laundering Reporting Officers) — needed full auditability and ability to generate regulatory reports; (5) **Downstream consumer teams** (KYC, Transaction Monitoring, EDD, Anomaly Index) — needed stable APIs, backward compatibility, and clear migration timelines. **Secondary stakeholders**: Regulatory affairs, Enterprise Risk, Finance (budget approval), and IT operations (infrastructure/deployment).

**How did you align competing interests?**
Key conflicts emerged: Compliance wanted complete auditability (logging everything); Engineering wanted lean, fast systems (minimal logging overhead). Resolution: **asynchronous audit trails** — real-time scoring path was optimized for speed, while audit logging happened asynchronously without blocking transactions. Another conflict: analysts wanted maximum rule expressiveness; engineering wanted simple rule schemas. Resolution: **invest in the DSL** — we built a domain-specific language that translated analyst mental models into executable logic, enabling expressiveness without burdening engineers with unbounded complexity. The biggest conflict: downstream consumers needed Cadence's APIs; GCIP's new schema was more efficient but incompatible. Resolution: **dual-write compatibility layer** — for 6 months, GCIP wrote to both legacy and new schemas, giving teams time to migrate. This added technical complexity but eliminated the false choice between "modernization" and "stability."

**What was your communication strategy?**
Three-tier communication: (1) **Executive level (Director of Compliance, Engineering Lead, Finance)** — monthly steering meetings with 1-page decks: status, risks, milestones, upcoming decisions. Language: business impact, regulatory confidence, timeline predictability; (2) **Practitioner level (analysts, MLRO teams)** — bi-weekly working sessions demoing features, gathering feedback, and celebrating incremental wins. Language: workflow efficiency, control/safety, and ease-of-use; (3) **Engineering coordination (downstream teams)** — monthly API/integration meetings with clear migration timelines and backward compatibility commitments. Language: technical specifications, API contracts, deprecation timelines. I also sent weekly written updates to all stakeholders (2 pages max) documenting progress, upcoming decisions, and asks—creating a shared, transparent narrative.

**How did you build trust?**
Trust came through three mechanisms: (1) **Underpromise, overdeliver** — I made conservative timeline estimates and consistently delivered early or on-time. When I said "sandbox will launch in 2 weeks," we launched in 10 days. Repeated wins built credibility; (2) **Transparency about risks** — I was explicitly honest about challenges (e.g., "latency testing revealed a caching issue; here's the mitigation, here's the new timeline"). This honesty prevented surprises and showed I wasn't hiding problems; (3) **User involvement in design** — compliance practitioners weren't told "here's the new system"; they were asked "what would make this better?" and saw their feedback implemented. Sandbox's "draft/published" states came from analyst feedback. This co-ownership drove adoption; (4) **Quick wins early** — first MVP delivered value (sandbox testing) before larger features (full rule configuration). Analysts could test changes in sandbox immediately, building confidence that the larger system would deliver on its promises; (5) **Awards and recognition** — my Leadership Award and 6000 Blue Rewards weren't just personal—I attributed success publicly to team members, the engineering lead's leadership, and the Director of Compliance's vision. Shared credit built team cohesion and stakeholder confidence.
